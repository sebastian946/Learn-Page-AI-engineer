# How to read an AI Engineer job posting

A job posting is a requirements document written by several people with different goals: the hiring manager, the team, HR and sometimes a recruiter who copied half of it from another posting. Your job is the same as when you get a vague user story: extract the real requirements, separate must-haves from noise and spot the risks.

This lesson gives you a repeatable method and walks through a fictional posting end to end.

## The four-pass method

Read every posting four times, quickly, each time looking for one thing.

### Pass 1 — What family is it?

Use the previous lesson. Is this AI-first, AI-support or ML? Look at the first three responsibilities, not the title. If it is clearly an ML training role, stop here.

### Pass 2 — What will you actually do every day?

Rewrite the responsibilities as verbs and objects. "Drive innovation in generative AI" means nothing. "Build retrieval pipelines over internal policy documents" means a lot. Keep only the concrete ones. These tell you which portfolio project to show.

### Pass 3 — Must-have vs nice-to-have

Postings mix hard requirements and wish lists. Signals that something is a **must-have**:

- It appears in both the responsibilities and the requirements.
- It is listed early under "Requirements" or "What you bring".
- It uses strong wording: "required", "proven experience", "you have shipped".
- It is tied to the product: a RAG product posting that mentions vector search is not joking.

Signals that something is a **nice-to-have**:

- It sits under "Bonus", "Preferred", "Nice to have", "Plus".
- It is one item in a long list of tools: "LangChain, LlamaIndex, Haystack, DSPy, CrewAI...". They want one, not all.
- It is a buzzword with no responsibility attached.

A practical rule: if you match roughly 60–70% of the must-haves and can tell a credible story for the gaps, apply. Postings describe an ideal candidate who rarely exists.

### Pass 4 — Red flags and questions

Note anything that worries you. You will turn these into interview questions.

## Decoding the keywords

Postings use shorthand. Here is what common phrases usually mean in practice.

| Posting says | Usually means |
|---|---|
| "LLM integration" | Calling provider APIs from a backend: streaming, retries, cost and rate-limit handling |
| "Prompt engineering" | System prompts, structured outputs, few-shot, prompt versioning and tests |
| "RAG experience" | Chunking, embeddings, vector search, re-ranking, citations, retrieval evals |
| "Agentic workflows" | Tool calling, LangGraph or similar, multi-step tasks, human-in-the-loop |
| "Evaluate model performance" | Golden datasets, LLM-as-judge, metrics, regression testing in CI |
| "Productionize" | Docker, CI/CD, cloud deploy, monitoring, SLOs |
| "Responsible AI" / "guardrails" | Content filtering, PII handling, prompt-injection defense, audit logs |
| "Fast-paced environment" | Small team, loose specs, frequent priority changes |
| "Full-stack AI engineer" | Backend plus some frontend, usually React or Next.js |
| "Experience with LLMOps" | Tracing, prompt and model versioning, eval pipelines, cost dashboards |

## Red flags

Not every bad sign is a deal-breaker. But each one deserves a question.

- **The title says AI Engineer, the requirements say PhD and PyTorch internals.** Mismatch. It is an ML research role.
- **Every AI tool ever released is required.** Nobody has five years of LangGraph. The team does not know what it needs yet.
- **No mention of evaluation, testing or quality anywhere.** They may be shipping demos. Could be an opportunity for you, could be chaos.
- **"Build our AI strategy" for a single engineer with no team.** You would be the whole AI department. Know that before you accept.
- **Unrealistic year counts.** "5+ years of LLM experience" in a field this young signals copied requirements.
- **No data access story.** An "internal knowledge assistant" role with no mention of where the data lives often means months of blocked work.

## Worked example

Here is a fictional posting. Read it once before you look at the breakdown.

```text
Senior AI Engineer — Brightpath Lending (Remote, US/LatAm)

Brightpath is a digital mortgage lender. We are adding AI to our loan
origination platform to help underwriters and support agents.

What you will do
- Design and build a retrieval-augmented assistant over our policy
  manuals, underwriting guidelines and product docs.
- Integrate LLMs (Anthropic, OpenAI via AWS Bedrock) into our Python
  services with streaming responses.
- Build evaluation pipelines to measure answer accuracy and catch
  regressions before release.
- Deploy and operate services on AWS (ECS, Lambda) with Docker and
  GitHub Actions.
- Partner with compliance to make sure answers are traceable and cited.

What you bring
- 5+ years of software engineering, mostly Python.
- Hands-on experience building RAG systems in production.
- Experience with vector databases (pgvector, OpenSearch or similar).
- Strong testing mindset; experience with automated testing and CI/CD.
- Familiarity with LLM evaluation (Ragas, DeepEval or custom metrics).

Nice to have
- LangGraph or other agent frameworks.
- Experience in financial services or another regulated industry.
- Fine-tuning experience.
- Databricks.
```

### Breakdown

**Pass 1 — Family.** AI-support. Established lender, AI added to an existing platform. Good fit.

**Pass 2 — Daily work.** Build a RAG assistant over policy PDFs. Integrate Bedrock models into Python services. Build eval pipelines. Deploy on AWS. Work with compliance on citations.

**Pass 3 — Must-haves.**

1. Python backend engineering. In both sections. You have it.
2. RAG in production. In both sections. Module 2 capstone (`mortgage-knowledge-assistant`) is almost a copy of this job.
3. Vector databases. pgvector is named. Module 2.
4. Testing mindset, CI/CD. Your core strength. Lead with it.
5. LLM evaluation. Ragas and DeepEval named. Module 3.
6. AWS deploy with Docker and GitHub Actions. Module 5.

Nice-to-haves: LangGraph (Module 4), regulated industry (your QA work in any regulated domain counts), fine-tuning and Databricks (Module 6). None is required.

**Pass 4 — Red flags and questions.** "5+ years of software engineering" is reasonable, not "5 years of LLMs". No major red flags. Questions to ask: Where do the policy documents live and how often do they change? Who owns the eval dataset? What is the latency target for underwriters? How is compliance sign-off done today?

**Verdict.** Strong match. The portfolio pieces to show: the Module 2 capstone and the Module 3 eval harness. The story: "I built a cited RAG assistant over mortgage policies, with a 50+ question golden set that gates every PR".

## Automate the first pass

When you are reading 30 postings a week, a structured extraction helps. Below, Claude extracts must-haves and nice-to-haves into a Pydantic model using forced tool use, then you compare against your own skills. Treat the output as a first draft: you still do pass 4 yourself.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

import asyncio
from pathlib import Path

import anthropic
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field

client = anthropic.AsyncAnthropic()


class PostingAnalysis(BaseModel):
    role_family: str = Field(description="ai-first | ai-support | ml")
    daily_work: list[str] = Field(description="Concrete verb + object tasks")
    must_have: list[str]
    nice_to_have: list[str]
    red_flags: list[str]


MY_SKILLS = {"python", "rag", "evals", "ci/cd", "docker", "aws", "pgvector"}


async def analyze(posting: str) -> PostingAnalysis:
    msg = await client.messages.create(
        model="claude-sonnet-5-5",
        max_tokens=1500,
        system="You analyze AI Engineer job postings for a senior SDET. Be literal; do not invent requirements.",
        tools=[{
            "name": "record_analysis",
            "description": "Record the structured analysis of a job posting.",
            "input_schema": PostingAnalysis.model_json_schema(),
        }],
        tool_choice={"type": "tool", "name": "record_analysis"},
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content": posting}],
    )
    tool_use = next(b for b in msg.content if b.type == "tool_use")
    return PostingAnalysis.model_validate(tool_use.input)


async def main() -> None:
    result = await analyze(Path("postings/brightpath.txt").read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
    gaps = [m for m in result.must_have if not any(s in m.lower() for s in MY_SKILLS)]
    print(result.model_dump_json(indent=2))
    print("Possible gaps:", gaps)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    asyncio.run(main())
```

The gap check is deliberately naive substring matching. It is a prompt for your own judgment, not a verdict.

## Common mistakes

- **Not applying because you miss one must-have.** If you cover most of them and can learn the rest, apply.
- **Applying to everything with "AI" in the title.** Filter by family first. A targeted application with a matching project beats ten generic ones.
- **Reading only the requirements.** Responsibilities tell you what they will ask in the interview. Requirements tell you what the resume filter checks.
- **Ignoring the domain.** A fintech posting cares about compliance and citations. A support platform cares about latency and cost per ticket. Tailor your examples.
- **Trusting automated extraction blindly.** A model can merge "nice to have" into "must have". Always skim the original.

## How this shows up in interviews

<InterviewQuestion question="Why are you interested in this role specifically?" level="mid">

Tie the answer to pass 2 and pass 3. Example for the Brightpath posting: "Three things in the posting match what I have been building. You need a cited RAG assistant over policy documents; I built one over mortgage policies with hybrid retrieval and citations. You want evaluation pipelines that catch regressions before release; that is my background as an SDET, and my project has a golden set of more than 50 questions running in GitHub Actions. And you deploy on AWS with Docker and CI/CD, which I have done for years on the testing side. I also like that this is a regulated domain: traceability and test evidence matter, and that is where I am strongest."

</InterviewQuestion>

<Callout type="interview" title="Turn red flags into good questions">

"How do you measure answer quality today?" is a polite way to probe a missing eval story. "Who else works on AI features?" probes the one-person-AI-department risk. Interviewers remember candidates who ask sharp questions.

</Callout>
