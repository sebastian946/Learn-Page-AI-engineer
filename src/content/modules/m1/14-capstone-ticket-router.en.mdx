## Company context

Cartwheel Outfitters is a fictional mid-size online retailer of outdoor gear (tents, boots, jackets, climbing equipment) shipping to the US and Canada. It processes around 9,000 orders a day and receives about **40,000 support tickets a month** by email and chat, in English and Spanish. Volume doubles in November and December.

Today, a team of 6 "triage agents" reads every ticket and moves it to one of seven queues in their helpdesk. Median time-to-queue is 3.5 hours, and over 20 hours on Mondays and during peak season. Misrouted tickets bounce between teams. Customers with safety issues (a stove that leaked gas, a harness with a frayed strap) sometimes wait a full day before a specialist sees them.

The company already uses Claude through the Anthropic API for internal tools, and has an OpenAI account through a marketing vendor. Their platform team wants nothing that locks them into one provider.

## Business goal

Automatically classify and route incoming tickets in **under a minute**, with accuracy at least as good as the human triage team, so the 6 triage agents can move to resolving tickets. Uncertain or high-risk tickets must go to a human review queue rather than be guessed. Leadership wants proof, with numbers, that the system is accurate, cheap and safe before turning it on.

## Functional requirements

1. **Classification with structured output.** For each ticket, produce a validated object:
   - `category`: one of `order_status`, `shipping_delay`, `return_refund`, `damaged_item`, `billing`, `account_access`, `fraud_report`, `product_question`, `other`
   - `priority`: `low`, `medium`, `high`, `urgent`
   - `safety_flag`: boolean (injury, fire, gas, structural failure of safety gear)
   - `language`: `en`, `es`, or `other`
   - `order_id`: string matching `A-12345`, or null
   - `confidence`: number between 0 and 1
   - `rationale`: one short sentence (for agents, not for customers), no PII
2. **Routing.** Map category to queue: logistics (`order_status`, `shipping_delay`), returns (`return_refund`, `damaged_item`), payments (`billing`), accounts (`account_access`), trust_safety (`fraud_report`, and anything with `safety_flag`), presales (`product_question`), general (`other`).
3. **Fallback strategy (model cascade).**
   - Tier 1: classify with the cheap model (`claude-haiku-5-5`, logical name `fast`).
   - Escalate to Tier 2 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`, logical name `smart`) when: confidence is below a **calibrated** threshold, validation needed a repair, or the predicted category is high-risk (`fraud_report`, `billing`) or `safety_flag` is true.
   - Send to the **human review queue** when Tier 2 confidence is still below its threshold, both tiers disagree on a high-risk category, or both tiers fail.
   - If all providers are unavailable, route to `general` with a `needs_review` tag. Never drop a ticket.
4. **Provider failover.** If Anthropic is unavailable (after retries), Tier 1 fails over to an OpenAI model configured in `models.toml`, which must have passed the same eval.
5. **Batch and single mode.** `route_ticket(ticket)` for one ticket, and a CLI that processes a JSONL file concurrently with progress and resumability.
6. **Cost report.** A command that reads the cost log and produces `reports/cost_report.md`: total spend, cost per 1k tickets, split by tier and provider, escalation rate, human queue rate, overhead from retries and repairs, and a monthly forecast at 40k and 80k tickets.
7. **Evaluation report.** A command that runs the labeled test set and produces `reports/eval_report.md` with per-category precision/recall/F1, macro-F1, routing accuracy, safety/fraud recall, confusion matrix and calibration table.

## Data

Cartwheel cannot share real tickets. Create a **synthetic but realistic labeled dataset** of at least 300 tickets:

- Cover all 9 categories, with realistic imbalance (order status and shipping delay are about half of real traffic).
- At least 15% in Spanish, plus some mixed-language tickets.
- Include hard cases: sarcasm, two issues in one ticket, missing order ids, a refund dispute that is really fraud, a "the zipper broke" that is `damaged_item` and a "the stove leaked gas" that is `safety_flag`.
- Label each ticket with category, priority, safety flag and queue. Have a second pass where you re-label 30 tickets blind and compute your own agreement: that is your human baseline.
- Split into **calibration (100)** and **test (200)**. Thresholds are tuned only on calibration. The test set is touched once per prompt version.

## Out of scope

- Generating replies to customers (a later module covers response drafting).
- Integrating with a real helpdesk API. Simulate queues as output fields or a local SQLite table.
- A web UI. A CLI and JSON outputs are enough. A FastAPI endpoint is optional (module 5 covers serving).
- Fine-tuning any model.
- Attachments and images.

Implementation notes:

- **Confidence is not calibrated by default.** A model saying 0.9 is not right 90% of the time. Bucket calibration-set predictions by stated confidence (for example 0–0.5, 0.5–0.7, 0.7–0.85, 0.85–1.0), compute actual accuracy per bucket, and pick the Tier 1 threshold as the lowest bucket edge where accuracy meets your target. Show the table in the eval report.
- **Choose thresholds by cost/accuracy curve.** Sweep the Tier 1 threshold and plot escalation rate, cost per 1k and macro-F1. Pick the knee, and justify it in the README.
- **High-risk rules are code, not prompt.** Escalation for fraud, billing and safety is decided in Python from the structured output, so a prompt change cannot weaken it.
- **Effort is a lever.** Try Tier 1 at low effort and Tier 2 at medium effort. Measure, don't assume.
- **Pre-processing.** Mask emails and phone numbers before sending to the model where the task does not need them, and truncate very long email threads to the latest message plus a short history.

## How to present this in an interview

Use a 3-minute story, then let the interviewer dig in.

1. **Problem in business terms.** "40k tickets a month, 3.5-hour median time-to-queue, safety tickets waiting a day. Goal: under a minute, as accurate as humans, safe by design."
2. **Architecture in one sentence.** "A cheap model classifies with structured output; low confidence, repairs or high-risk categories escalate to a stronger model; anything still uncertain goes to humans; everything runs through my provider-agnostic client with retries, rate limits and cost logging."
3. **The numbers.** Macro-F1, safety recall, routing accuracy, human queue rate, cost per 1k tickets, p95 latency. Say which targets you missed, if any, and why.
4. **The interesting decision.** Calibration. "The model's stated confidence was overconfident in the 0.7–0.85 band, so I set the threshold at 0.85 using the calibration set. That raised escalations from 9% to 14% but brought routing accuracy above target, and cost stayed under budget."
5. **What you'd do next.** Online monitoring of human override rate per prompt version, a CI eval gate (module 3), and serving it behind an API with a queue (module 5).

Expect follow-ups such as: "Why not just use the strong model for everything?" (show the cost/accuracy curve), "How do you know your synthetic data is representative?" (data card, hard cases, plan to validate on real samples), "What happens when the provider is down?" (failover and degraded mode, with tests), and "How would you detect drift?" (category distribution and confidence shifts per week, override rate). Have the eval and cost reports open; numbers you can show beat numbers you remember.
