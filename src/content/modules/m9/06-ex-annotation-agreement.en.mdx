## Goal

In Module 3 you labeled 30 items alone and checked one judge against your own labels. Now you run a small but real human evaluation program and hold an LLM judge to the human standard:

1. Write a versioned **annotation guideline** for two criteria on support-bot answers: `correct` (yes/no/cannot_judge) and `helpfulness` (anchored 1–4).
2. Collect labels from **at least three raters** on 60 items.
3. Measure agreement properly: Cohen's kappa per pair, quadratic-weighted kappa and Krippendorff's alpha (ordinal) for helpfulness, all with bootstrap CIs.
4. **Adjudicate** disagreements, update the guideline to v2 with new borderline examples, and relabel a fresh batch to show whether agreement improved.
5. Run a two-model **judge jury** and compare judge-vs-gold kappa with human-vs-human kappa, per segment.

This is folder `02-annotation/` of `llm-eval-lab`.

## Context

Use answers from any support assistant you built (the Module 5 `support-assistant-prod` bot, or the Module 1 ticket router extended with a reply step). You need 60 items for round 1 and 30 fresh items for round 2. Include at least two segments, for example `billing` vs `technical`, or `short` vs `long` conversations. Make sure roughly a third of the answers have real problems.

About raters: ask two colleagues or friends to label. If you truly cannot get two other people, use one other person plus **yourself again after at least five days**, without looking at your first labels. Report that as intra-rater reliability and say so clearly. Do not use LLMs as "raters" for the human agreement study; that defeats the purpose.

Label file format, one row per (item, rater) in `labels/round1.jsonl`:

<Hints>
  <Hint>Write the borderline examples first, before the definitions feel "done". Pick 4–6 answers from your pool that you yourself hesitated on. For each, write the decision and one sentence of reasoning. Those examples will move your kappa more than polishing definitions.</Hint>
  <Hint>Bootstrap agreement by resampling columns (items) of the raters-by-items matrix, never individual labels. Krippendorff's alpha can return nan on a resample with no variation; skip those and report how many you skipped.</Hint>
  <Hint>Specific agreement makes the kappa paradox visible. Positive agreement is `2 * both_yes / (2 * both_yes + disagreements)`, and negative agreement is the same with `no`. If positive agreement is 0.95 and negative agreement is 0.50, your raters agree on easy passes and disagree on failures, which is where the risk lives.</Hint>
  <Hint>For the judge comparison, compute human-human kappa as the mean of the three pairwise kappas, and judge-gold kappa against the adjudicated label. Then compute judge-vs-each-rater kappa too. If the jury agrees with each rater about as well as raters agree with each other, it behaves like another rater. Break all of this down by segment; a jury that is fine on billing and weak on technical answers is a conditional "yes".</Hint>
