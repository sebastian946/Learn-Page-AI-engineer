## Company context

Harborline Home Lending is a fictional mid-sized US mortgage lender. It originates conventional, FHA and VA loans in 14 states, with about 220 loan officers, 40 underwriters and a 15-person operations team. Harborline sells most of its loans to the secondary market, so every file must follow both its internal policies and the investor guidelines it has adopted.

Today, policy knowledge lives in a mess of places:

- A 200+ page internal **Underwriting Guide** (PDF, updated quarterly, with tables of DTI/LTV limits).
- About 60 **policy bulletins** (PDF and HTML), each superseding parts of the guide from an effective date.
- A **product matrix** per loan program (PDF tables).
- An internal **FAQ wiki** (HTML) maintained by operations, partly outdated.

Loan officers send roughly 300 policy questions a week to an internal Slack channel. Underwriters answer them between files. The median response time is 3 hours, and answers are not always consistent. Two recent post-close audit findings were traced to an officer acting on an outdated bulletin.

## Business goal

Cut the median time to a correct policy answer from hours to under a minute, while reducing underwriter interruptions by at least 40% within one quarter of launch. The assistant must make audits easier, not riskier: every answer is traceable to specific, current policy text.

## Functional requirements

1. **Ingestion** of the Underwriting Guide, bulletins, product matrices (PDF) and FAQ pages (HTML). Extract text and tables, keep section numbers, page numbers and titles.
2. **Document versioning:** every document row carries `doc_type`, `jurisdiction`, `effective_date`, `is_current` and `content_hash`, the same metadata contract as `rag-lab`. By default retrieval only returns current versions; an `as_of` date selects the version that was in force on that date. Superseded versions stay in the database for audit.
3. **Metadata filters:** jurisdiction (`US` plus the officer's state, such as `US-TX`), loan program (conventional, FHA, VA) and document type, applied inside each retrieval leg before fusion. They come from the request context first; the assistant may refine them from the question after validation.
4. **Hybrid retrieval:** pgvector cosine similarity plus Postgres full-text search, 40 candidates per leg, fused with RRF (k = 60).
5. **Re-ranking** of the fused candidates with a cross-encoder (local by default; a hosted re-ranker can be a configurable option).
6. **Query rewriting:** conversation condensation for follow-ups; multi-query optional behind a flag.
7. **Cited answers:** every factual sentence cites at least one chunk, showing document, section and page. Citations are validated before the answer is shown.
8. **Abstention:** when the corpus does not contain the answer, the assistant says so and links the closest sections. It never answers from general mortgage knowledge.
9. **API:** a FastAPI service with `POST /ask` (question, optional filters, optional history) returning answer, citations, `answer_found` and a `trace_id`. Streaming is a nice-to-have.
10. **Minimal UI:** a simple web page or Streamlit app showing the answer, clickable citations and the filters.
11. **Evaluation suite:** at least **50 golden Q&A pairs** reviewed against the source documents, including at least 8 unanswerable questions and at least 5 multi-turn follow-ups.
12. **Latency and cost dashboard:** per-request logs aggregated into p50/p95 latency by stage and cost per 1,000 requests.

## Non-functional requirements

| Area | Target |
|---|---|
| Latency | p95 under 4 s end-to-end for non-streaming answers; p95 retrieval (rewrite excluded) under 600 ms |
| Cost | Under a target you define per 1,000 requests, computed from logged token usage and current provider pricing; document the assumption |
| Faithfulness | ≥ 0.90 average on the golden set (Ragas or DeepEval), no answerable item below 0.5 without an explanation |
| Context recall | ≥ 0.85 average on answerable items |
| Retrieval | hit@5 ≥ 0.85 on items labelled with `expected_doc_id` and `expected_section` |
| Abstention | ≥ 90% of unanswerable items correctly answered "not found" |
| Citation validity | 100% of displayed citations point to retrieved chunks with matching quotes |
| Security | No borrower PII in the corpus or logs; secrets via environment variables; no data sent to providers without an approved agreement (document which providers you use) |
| Auditability | Every request has a trace: query, rewrites, filters, retrieved IDs with scores, prompt version, model, output, validation result |

## Out of scope

- Answering questions about specific borrowers or loan files.
- Making credit decisions or approving exceptions.
- Authentication and multi-tenant permissions (assume an internal network; Module 7 revisits this).
- Production deployment, CI/CD and load testing (Module 5 deploys this project).
- Full LLM-as-judge calibration and online monitoring (Module 3 builds this on top of your suite).

## Acceptance criteria

- `docker compose up` followed by `make ingest` and `make eval` works on a clean machine.
- Asking about a rule that a bulletin superseded returns the new rule and cites the bulletin, not the old guide section.
- Filtering by FHA never returns conventional-only chunks.
- The eval report meets the non-functional targets, or explains each miss with a diagnosis and a plan.
- The dashboard shows p50/p95 latency per stage (rewrite, retrieval, re-rank, generation) and cost per 1,000 requests for at least 200 logged requests.
- A reviewer can pick any answer in the logs and reconstruct how it was produced from its trace.

Notes:

- Reuse the framework-free core from `rag-lab/07-from-scratch/`, or the LangChain pipeline if you prefer. Justify the choice in an ADR.
- Put an exact-match response cache in front of generation, keyed by question, filters, corpus version and prompt version. Skip semantic caching for underwriting content and explain why.
- Use a small model (`claude-haiku-5-5`) for rewriting and a larger one (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) for generation. Put stable instructions in a cached prompt prefix.

## Suggested timeline (5 days)

**Day 1 — Corpus and ingestion.** Write the synthetic corpus, including superseding bulletins and tables. Build parsing, structure-aware chunking and metadata extraction. Load into pgvector with HNSW and full-text indexes. Start drafting golden questions while you write the documents; that is when you know the answers best.

**Day 2 — Retrieval.** Hybrid search with RRF, metadata and effective-date filters, cross-encoder re-ranking, conversation condensation. Build the retrieval benchmark (hit@5, MRR per category) on the golden items labelled with document and section, reusing the `rag-lab` golden set as a starting point, and tune fetch size and top-k.

**Day 3 — Generation and API.** Citation prompt, structured output, citation validation, abstention. FastAPI `POST /ask` with trace IDs and request logging. Response and embedding caches. Minimal UI.

**Day 4 — Evaluation.** Finish and review the 50+ golden pairs. Run Ragas or DeepEval with a pinned judge. Analyze the worst items, fix the top issues, re-run. Write the eval report.

**Day 5 — Dashboard and polish.** Generate at least 200 logged requests (golden set plus paraphrases), build the latency/cost dashboard, write ADRs, README, record the demo GIF. Do a clean-machine setup test.

## How to present this in an interview

Use a two-minute story, then let the interviewer dig in.

1. **Problem (20 s).** "A mortgage lender's loan officers waited hours for policy answers, and outdated bulletins caused audit findings."
2. **Approach (40 s).** "I built a cited RAG assistant: structure-aware chunking with effective-date metadata, hybrid pgvector plus full-text search fused with RRF, a cross-encoder re-ranker, and claim-level citations validated before display."
3. **Proof (40 s).** "I wrote 50+ golden questions with a domain-style review, including unanswerable and follow-up questions. Faithfulness, context recall and abstention are tracked per item." Quote your actual numbers.
4. **Operations (20 s).** "Every request is traced; the dashboard shows p95 by stage and cost per 1,000 requests. Prompt caching and fewer, re-ranked chunks cut input tokens." Again, your own measured numbers.

Be ready for follow-ups:

- *How do you handle superseded policies?* Effective-date metadata and filters, plus golden items that specifically test superseded rules.
- *Why not just use a bigger context window?* Cost, latency, "lost in the middle", and weaker citations. Show your measurements.
- *What failed first and how did you find it?* Pick a real worst-item from your report and walk through the stage-by-stage diagnosis.
- *What would you do next?* CI gate on faithfulness (Module 3), deployment with SLOs (Module 5), multi-tenant permissions (Module 7).

Open the repo during the interview. Show the trace of one answer, the eval report and one ADR. Concrete artifacts beat adjectives.
